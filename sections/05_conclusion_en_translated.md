# 4 Conclusion

This study investigated the potential roles of ATG4C and ATG4D in human antiviral immunity. We first performed population genetic analysis of both genes using the gnomAD v4.1 database. The results showed that nonsynonymous variants with high CADD scores exceeding the MSC95 threshold were generally rare in the population, with ATG4D exhibiting particularly pronounced variant constraint. This indicates that both genes are subject to purifying selection and that clearly damaging variants have low tolerance in the general population, consistent with the important functions of ATG4C and ATG4D in autophagy, cellular homeostasis maintenance, and multi-tissue expression.

At the same time, we identified a small number of rare, high-impact nonsynonymous variants in ATG4C and ATG4D, including ATG4C p.Arg389Ter and ATG4D Ser89Asn and Arg382His carried by patients with viral encephalitis. These variants have extremely low population frequencies and high predicted deleteriousness, suggesting potential functional impact. Because this study focuses on biallelic variants — where both alleles of the same gene carry potentially deleterious variants — this genetic configuration is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance or recessive susceptibility.

We further selected 16 ATG4C and 10 ATG4D homozygous nonsynonymous variants that were either relatively common in the population or predicted to be highly deleterious, overexpressed them in HEK293T cells, and examined their mRNA and protein expression by RT-qPCR and Western blotting. The results showed that relatively common ATG4C variants did not significantly affect transcript levels, mRNA stability or translation, produce truncated proteins, or reduce protein stability. However, four rare pLOF variants with high predicted deleteriousness — ATG4C Val320Glyfs*8, Trp203Leufs*4, Glu216Ter, and c.797-2A>C — showed no detectable protein expression, indicating that these variants likely cause loss of ATG4C protein through protein truncation, aberrant folding, or reduced stability, thereby significantly impairing gene function. This also validated the accuracy of pLOF, AlphaMissense, and CADD prediction tools for identifying deleterious ATG4C variants.

In contrast, all ATG4D variants showed detectable mRNA expression, and although some variants exhibited reduced mRNA levels, all tested ATG4D variants expressed full-length protein with molecular weight consistent with wild-type. This suggests that the potential functional impact of ATG4D variants is likely mediated not through protein loss, but rather through alterations in enzyme activity, substrate recognition, protein–protein interactions, or subcellular localization.

In summary, this study demonstrates that ATG4C and ATG4D are under evolutionary constraint, and that their rare, high-impact variants have potential functional significance. Some ATG4C loss-of-function variants cause loss of protein expression, suggesting severe functional impairment, whereas ATG4D variants, although not causing overt protein loss, may still contribute to disease susceptibility through effects on protein function. These results support the involvement of deleterious ATG4C and ATG4D variants in severe central nervous system viral infections such as viral encephalitis, and provide genetic and experimental evidence for further investigation of the role of ATG4-mediated autophagy in human antiviral immunity.
